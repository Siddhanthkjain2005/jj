# Build UNRULED around magic with consequences

**Recommendation checked 10 November 2026. Working title: UNRULED — The Last Lift.**

Build a **six-to-eight-minute mixed-reality rescue adventure** in which players borrow magical abilities from a miniature world and give them to other objects or their own hands. UNRULED is the strongest foundation in the shared chat: property transfer, the donor losing its ability, magical hands and the sky whale already belong to that proposal. Our contribution is to make those ingredients into a specific, testable game: three consistent powers, alternative solutions, forgiving recovery, and an ending that remembers the route the player actually invented. This is a recommendation for a separate Quest project; it is not implemented in the current racing APK. Its appeal and reliability still need testing. No concept can guarantee a prize, but this gives us a clear interaction to prove and a complete experience to finish. ## Give players a world they can understand and change

The promise is simple: **borrow magic; build a way home.** A tiny courier whale has crashed beside a broken sky village. Its lighthouse bell is stranded, the launch dock has failed, and the inhabitants need your help. The world fits comfortably in front of a seated player. You remain stationary while the little village changes around your actions.

The opening should teach through one visible consequence. A cloud carries a large FLOAT seal. Pinch its raised tab, peel it off, and attach it to the grounded whale. The whale rises with a delighted chirp; the cloud settles into a catch bowl and looks offended. The first correct action creates a funny, recoverable consequence. It never destroys a house or makes the player feel tricked.

Keep **FLOAT, BOUNCE and MAGNETIC**. Each seal has one owner; moving it takes the ability away from its donor. Compatible targets show a clear preview. Icons, shape and animation communicate the rules alongside color. Avoid promising that every object supports every combination: the first scene contains a small, deliberately designed set of targets whose behavior is consistent.

During the next two minutes, BOUNCE turns a padded hand-tool into a gentle launcher for a slow glowing seed. A fixed launch pad provides the same solution for players who prefer one-handed interaction. MAGNETIC then helps retrieve a metal route key, while the donor crane lowers its bridge section into a safe dock. Preview that consequence before the transfer. Every mistake should invite another attempt through a return tray, undo or a reachable reset.

The visual direction should retain the floating islands and luminous wonder you liked: **a richly detailed toy harbor, expressive creatures, tactile surfaces and selective glow**. Midnight blue, coral and warm gold give it a coherent identity. Spend the finest detail on the objects players touch. The peeled seal stretches briefly, releases with an elastic sound, and changes the source immediately. Spatial audio, tiny character reactions and animated machinery should make successful actions feel substantial. This is proposed art direction, not evidence of existing artwork or device performance.

## Make one puzzle support two genuine solutions

The final objective is concrete: deliver the correct bell through the lighthouse receiving ring and settle it in its catcher. A cradle holds the bell until the player configures a route. Its physical shape and release animation must explain why simply grabbing the bell is unavailable; an invisible restriction would weaken the design.

In the **lift-and-guide solution**, FLOAT raises the bell from the cradle into a bounded lane. MAGNETIC on a hand-tool lets the player guide it into the receiver’s broad approach. Removing FLOAT over the cup settles the bell and rings it. The challenge is sequencing and spatial placement, with unlimited retries.

In the **launch solution**, BOUNCE powers an adjustable pad below the cradle. A broad trajectory preview lets the player aim through the same ring before releasing the bell. A capped impulse sends it to the padded catcher. This is a designed physical puzzle, not an assurance that arbitrary physics will produce a solution. Both routes must be built and tested independently before connecting the story.

The ending should reward the solution itself. A successful lift leaves a little sky dock; a successful launch leaves a courier chute. **The whale uses the route you created in the closing animation.** Windows illuminate, the bell adds a note to the music, and the village becomes a warm inhabited garden. These are two bounded scene configurations driven by actual gameplay events. They make player authorship visible without requiring an unrestricted construction engine.

After the six-to-eight-minute story, offer one short remix with different starting seal locations or a missing launch pad. An optional personal best can count transfers; the first completion should not demand efficiency. This creates a reason to experiment again without inventing an endless-content claim. FAST, global gravity removal, online systems and additional worlds should wait until this small game earns voluntary replay.

## Prove the interaction before expanding the promise

You have no Quest headset. **That does not automatically prevent entry**, given the permitted simulator footage, but simulator success cannot establish hand comfort or hardware frame rate. Meta supports simulated head/hand input in Unity on Apple Silicon and recommends testing a device build on a headset before release. Arrange a borrowed device or a collaborator who can test the build as early as possible. [Meta testing guide](https://developers.meta.com/vr/documentation/unity/unity-deploy-and-test/).

The first gate should be one seal, two stationary objects, visible donor loss and undo. Then ask five unfamiliar headset testers to transfer a seal and explain what changed without coaching. This is formative feedback, not statistical proof. If peeling is awkward, keep the conservation puzzle and simplify the seal into a larger grab-and-place token. Test palm attachment separately: overlapping hands and tracking loss can break the intended magic. A fixed-pad alternative must remain usable.

Across the remaining development window, prioritize a runnable XR project and membership/access checks first; one-property interaction next; then two-property puzzles and early device feedback. Only after those work should the third property, complete ending and focused art pass follow. Reserve the final days for novice play, recovery checks, performance, a matching build and truthful demonstration capture. This is a conditional triage order, not a promise of eight-day production quality.

Its objective-validation and saved-story patterns can inform the new project, but its racing renderer and phone controls do not validate Quest interaction.
