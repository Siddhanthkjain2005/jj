# Project Apex strategy research

Prepared 9 November 2026. This note covers Parts 6–9 and 13 of the supplied brief. It proposes a business and production strategy around VelocityVerse / Season One: The Openline, an uncleared working franchise name. Design proposals and financial scenarios below are assumptions, not shipped capabilities, forecasts, legal clearance, or evidence of player demand. Competitive facts were checked against the linked official product, developer, regulator and publisher pages. Weaknesses/opportunities are our analysis of product scope, not measured player sentiment. The baseline is the repository at commit `09b24a0`: an offline WebGL simulation packaged in an Android WebView, not a completed Unity production game.

## What does the competitive evidence imply for differentiation?

### Takeaway

The opening is a small, causally connected loop: race; inspect a comprehensible trace from that actual attempt; make a crew decision; see the garage or district remember it. Original characters make the consequence matter. Racing, stories, customization, persistent spaces, co-building, teaching, clubs and creator economies already exist separately and often together. No researched evidence supports an unprecedented-feature or billion-dollar claim. The strongest proposed differentiation is the quality of this integration on an Android phone, with fair competition and authored character consequences.

### Inferences

**Three advantages to test rather than announce:**

1. **Evidence becomes drama.** Tavi's interpretation of a measured stop or corner changes an authored decision in Neri's story. The trace is not a science page beside a game. Criterion: players can explain how their driving changed the crew outcome without reading an external explanation.
2. **A small world remembers specific acts.** A repaired timing beacon, a newly open school bay, and Sola's response embody persistent choices. Criterion: players notice, remember and care about one consequence after a week. Persistence itself is established; the proposed specific integration is a meaningful combination/novel hypothesis to validate.
3. **Belonging without buying rank.** One comparable car, assist-separated records, cosmetic identity and capped contribution limits. Criterion: players trust competitive results and choose to help a crew even without a spend incentive. Fair cosmetics do not guarantee a viable business.

Do not characterize the opportunity as an empty market. F1 already has fictional drama, Forza now documents persistent shared spaces/co-building, Fortnite/Roblox have creator economies, and Minecraft/STEM Racing connect play and learning. The proposed defendability, if earned, would come from loved characters, a consistently authored canon, an economical content pipeline, trusted competition and a community with useful creations. It is not a defensible patent claim about a dashboard feature.

No same-device comparative playtest, independent sentiment study, representative retention benchmark, platform-wide mechanic census, trademark clearance, downloadable-count-to-active-user inference or market-size estimate was conducted. Official marketing descriptions establish offered scope, not delivered quality. Exact live prices/features remain region/version dependent. No unsupported claim about its cancellation or future support is made.

## What architecture and AI boundaries fit the first product and later scale?

### Takeaway

Keep the existing Android prototype as a learning and simulation reference; run a short measured production-engine spike before committing to the port. Proposed production default is Unity 6.3 LTS/URP, a pure C# simulation and an offline-capable mission client. Add one modular backend and asynchronous verified ghosts only when community testing requires it. Real-time multiplayer, public creation and AI narrative each need separate proof and operating capacity.

### Cited Findings

Unity's current support page identifies 6.3 LTS support through December 2027. Its 2026 pricing notice lists Personal eligibility up to $200,000 revenue/funding and Pro at $2,310/seat/year prepaid or $210 monthly, with applicable threshold/terms. URP supports Vulkan and OpenGL ES among other APIs. Unreal has mobile forward/deferred paths with explicit performance/fidelity tradeoffs. Epic's standard game license charges 5% on qualifying lifetime gross above $1 million, subject to exclusions/terms; non-game seat pricing is a separate case. These facts do not measure this game's performance. [Unity support](https://unity.com/releases/unity-6/support), [2026 pricing](https://unity.com/products/pricing-updates), [URP compatibility](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.3/manual/render-pipelines/universal-render-pipeline/requirements), [Unreal mobile rendering](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/mobile-rendering-and-shading-modes-for-unreal-engine), [Epic license](https://www.unrealengine.com/license).

### Inferences

**Decision process (proposal).** Compare the current WebView reference and a Unity spike for 10 working days using the same car, one representative circuit sector, touch controls and one high-art garage. Unity is the preferred path because a focused C#/URP production and asset workflow fits the proposed small team, not because an engine brand guarantees premium quality. If the team already has strong Unreal expertise, evaluate it with the same mobile criteria; no parallel long-term engine teams. Lock a stable editor/package patch and source-controlled settings after the spike. Do not promise Unity native performance before a real device build exists.

**Client boundaries.** Separate deterministic-in-one-runtime simulation stepping, input, assist policy, race rules, AI driving, mission evaluation, narrative state, presentation and persistence. A `MissionOutcome` contains mission ID/version, attempt ID, simulation/config hashes, measured predicates, authored choice and result, not an LLM-created reward. The mission checks actual speed, braking/corner telemetry, ordered checkpoints and eligible attempt state; resets/teleports invalidate relevant objectives. Store event → outcome → local save atomically so an app kill cannot award twice or lose a completed choice. Keep accessibility assist states explicit rather than labeling a player inferior.

**Simulation handoff.** Port pure `src/sim` behavior/data to C#, preserving SI units, fixed step and rendering interpolation. Use known acceleration, stopping, grip/slalom/barrier and lap fixtures as comparisons. Do not silently replace the model with an engine wheel component or regenerate golden traces to hide divergence. Native float differences require an agreed tolerance investigation. Cross-device exact determinism is unproven; online validation must pin a trusted simulation/build/config version.

**Backend shape.** Start with one modular monolith: identity/consent, profile, crew, mission/career, ghost validation, content, moderation and commerce modules with clear interfaces. A relational database is appropriate for ownership, memberships, outcome events and entitlements; object storage holds ghost blobs and reviewed assets; CDN holds immutable content manifests. Use a job worker from the same codebase for replay validation/moderation scans. Add a queue when measured workload/backpressure requires it. A cache or separate service is a response to a demonstrated bottleneck or isolation need, not a global-platform diagram prerequisite.

**Minimal data model.** `Account` and separate `Identity/Consent` records; `Profile`; `CharacterBond(characterId, chapterVersion, authoredFlags)`; `MissionAttempt`; append-only `OutcomeEvent`; `Crew/Membership`; `Season/Contribution`; `Ghost(runId, simHash, configHash, blobHash, verificationState)`; `Creation/version/reviewState`; `Entitlement/receipt`; `Report/action/appeal`. Keep identifying records separate from display profile and analytics. Cloud upload uses revision checks; do not last-write-wins merge duplicate XP or story branches from two devices. Conflict UI preserves local progress and explains which story choice is adopted.

**Security and reliability.** Managed authentication using modern supported flows, short-lived access tokens and Android secure token storage; TLS; least-privilege service roles; secret management; encrypted backups and tested restores; rate limits; request size/content limits; schema validation; malicious text rendered as text. Never trust the client to grant currency, purchased content, rank or competitive lap times. Every mutation accepts an idempotency key and validates ownership. Receipt verification and refund/revocation operate on the backend with an audit trail. Keep deployment rollback/config kill switches and tested schema migrations. A staff moderation/commerce tool requires role-based access and an audit log. Schedule dependency/SDK inventory reviews. A local guest save stays usable if online services fail.

**World memory without MMO cost.** Personal flags affect a local authored district. Crew flags are a bounded aggregate of validated contributions. Global event outcomes use one versioned, scheduled snapshot produced by rules and reviewed by an editor; clients download the resulting content/config. No permanently simulated global population is necessary. Players who miss an event get an archived mission, not permanent exclusion. Canon branches should reconverge at deliberate authored points while preserving smaller personal memories, containing narrative content cost.

**Asynchronous competition before real time.** Upload a control stream and metadata, not only the declared finish time. Server reruns the pinned model, checks ordered checkpoints/bounds/assist class and produces its own result. Mark unverified older runs as local records. Sign manifests and verify asset hashes, but client signatures/Play Integrity signals alone are not authority. Rate-limit expensive replay validation and separate pending from accepted results. Use one best attempt per bounded crew slot with capped participation; retries improve mastery rather than infinite grind.

**Real-time model.** Server owns race state, contacts, rules, checkpoints, weather/config and results. Local client predicts its own input; snapshots correct it, rival cars interpolate. Bound correction instead of hiding impossible teleports. First test at 8 cars with proposed 20–30Hz network snapshots and an internally profiled simulation frequency; choose rates from traces, not convention. Exercise 80/150/250ms RTT, jitter, 2–5% packet loss, disconnect/rejoin and malicious controls. Matchmaking separates compatible build/config/assist/rules and offers explicitly identified AI while queues are sparse. No fake human opponents. Dedicated race workers can be separated later because latency/isolation needs differ from profile API work; this is a justified boundary.

**Operations and measurement.** Proposed targets, not measured facts: reference Android sustained60fps, explicitly labeled30fps minimum tier; frame-time p50/p95/p99 and thermal behavior over20min; <=750MB resident memory; <=250MB initial package; <=8s cold launch; <=5s race load. Instrument simulation/render/asset load separately, app lifecycle interruption, shader failures and save recovery. Record device/GPU/API/preset/build, not “works on Android.” Starting cloud metrics: verified-run cost/time, queue depth, restore success, p95 API latency, moderation backlog, report handling and access failures. Client events should be a narrow allowlist with documented retention/purpose; no raw chat, precise location, contact list, face/voice embeddings or advertising identifier.

**AI investment order.** Existing rule-based opponent driving and objective evaluation are already meaningful AI without an LLM. The Openline needs believable consequences and useful coaching before autonomous talking characters.

**Runtime generation boundary.** Default to no runtime free-text dialogue, voice chat, image generation or personal-memory embeddings for minors. An AI tool cannot access payments, contact invitations, private child data, authoritative scores or safety decisions. Treat creator text as untrusted input; a generative model has no tool execution permission. Approved prompts/outputs and content versions are recorded; deploy a kill switch and fallback. Proposed experiment cap: <=$0.02 per optional generated-session summary and <=3s response; disable if either budget or safety review fails. These are hypothetical caps, not current provider price/performance. Moderation, localization and editorial review can cost more than inference.

## Can the business be ethical, financeable and legally operable?

### Takeaway

The initial payer should be an adult who likes the game or buys a clearly priced story/cosmetic product for a supervised young person. Do not plan the company around children spending, ads or a marketplace before players want the core experience. A small crew-driven Android game may become a viable studio; a global franchise is an upside scenario that must be earned through retention, character affinity, production economics and safe operations.

### Inferences

**Audience and launch proposal.** Creative target: teens and adults who enjoy cars, inventive teams and character stories, with later family accessibility. Initial account/online pilot in India should recruit verified consenting adults18+; test teen/family usability separately under a reviewed research/guardian protocol. This is a product-risk choice, not a legal opinion or permission to ignore other rules. A commercial13+ experience needs jurisdiction-specific age assurance, consent, advertising/analytics, UGC, consumer-law and store review; under13 expansion needs its own approved design and operating plan. Do not simultaneously market the first account product to6-year-olds, teen open-world players, esports veterans and schools. School participation is not a blanket consent exemption.

**Revenue sequence and payer.**

Avoid paid performance, loot boxes, cash betting, tradable speculative assets, streak penalties, fictitious countdown discounts, infinite-spend team scores and push notifications exploiting a child's relationship with a character. Keep plain currency/prices and guardian purchase limits where needed. Character affection must not be used to guilt a child into buying help for an endangered crew member. Accessible learning does not depend on subscription.

**Creator economics gate.** Begin with free approved challenge sharing; no cash economy while discovery, quality or moderation is weak. Later hypothetical split: creator receives70% of **eligible net content receipts after stated store/payment fees and refunds**, studio30%, with a clear example and separately disclosed taxes/withholding. Not a70%-of-customer-spend promise. Require adult/guardian contracting as applicable, payout identity/tax checks, fraud review, rights warranties, appeals and transparent discovery; do not pay only for addictive session minutes. Approved game-component creation should have low upload/resource budgets. No arbitrarily executable scripts in first public editor.

**IP and brand operations.** “Project Apex,” “VelocityVerse,” chapter/character/team names are working labels. Search/trademark review in target jurisdictions, language/confusability testing and store/domain availability precede marketing investment; an original-sounding name is not cleared. Keep human concept sketches, revisions, character turnarounds and source records, with signed employee/contractor assignment, voice/music/font/texture/model licenses and permissions for adaptations/merchandise. Generative asset availability does not itself guarantee exclusivity, copyright protection or third-party clearance. Review resemblance of character combinations and car body shapes as well as logos. Never lift F1 branding, real driver faces, competitor UI art or branded circuit shapes into the game without rights. UGC terms should grant only the rights needed to host/promote content, explain optional adaptation deals and provide notice/takedown/appeal. Maintain a canon owner, asset ledger and versioned story bible before outside animation/license partners.

**Critical founder verdict.** This is currently an ambitious game concept with a useful working prototype, not evidence of a billion-dollar opportunity. A large outcome is possible only if the studio repeatedly creates entertaining content, retains paying adults/families, develops recognizable original characters and safely operates communities at sustainable cost. Nothing researched establishes that demand. The hardest technical problem is achieving fair, responsive connected play and persistent creations across heterogeneous phones with an economical content and safety pipeline; the hardest commercial problem is giving people a reason to choose an unknown IP repeatedly. Better bloom and more menu cards do not answer either.

Players may leave because steering feels unpredictable, loads/heat interrupt sessions, objectives are unclear, dialogue delays racing, outcomes feel fake, progression demands chores, a cosmetic shop obscures fun, friends cannot meaningfully help or moderation fails. A tiny first district with authentic consequences can expose these failures earlier than10empty cities. The main production bottleneck will likely be authored assets/story/testing throughput, not raw database capacity.

**A small founding team can plausibly build:** one polished Android district/car/circuit; four expressive characters with limited animation; one authored mission chain; understandable driving/lab feedback; robust local saves; cosmetic identity; a measured native-production spike. With6full-time people and targeted contractors, a90day improved first playable is an ambitious planning target conditional on existing prototype reuse, staffing and limited art/voice scope. It is not a promise of a native AAA release. Assign production/creative design, gameplay/simulation, client/UI, technical art/environment, character/story/animation and QA/research ownership; part-time audio/accessibility/privacy specialists still need budget. Backend/multiplayer or a full engine migration consumes capacity that must displace other work.

**Absolutely defer:** seamless MMO geography, unrestricted chat/child AI companions, live generative canon, a general-purpose engine/editor, player-traded speculative currencies, full car/engine manufacturing simulation, ten simultaneous cities, TV production, physical inventory, and real-time esports infrastructure before a stable8car pilot. AI should assist internal production and structured coaching; it is not a substitute for writers, artists, safeguarding or reliable physics.

**Before significant spending:** (1) observe first-session steering and objective comprehension on target phones; (2) compare the race→trace→crew consequence loop with race-only control in a small randomized adult research pilot; (3) ask for unaided character recall and next-story choice after a delay; (4) measure voluntaryD1/D7/D30 return with defined cohorts, not push/reward coercion; (5) test willingness to pay using a real clear offer, never fictitious purchases; (6) profile20minute device sessions; (7) demonstrate save recovery and authoritative ghost validation before leaderboard rollout; (8) rehearse abuse/report response and creator rights review before public social content. Set stop/pivot criteria before results: if the combined loop reduces enjoyment or return, simplify it; if only racing works, make a better focused racing game rather than force a franchise narrative. If characters are remembered but nobody likes driving, test a smaller engineering/story format rather than building another city.
