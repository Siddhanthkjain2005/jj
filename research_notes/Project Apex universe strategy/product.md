# Product systems, Android UX and first playable

Research/design notes for Project Apex, 9 November 2026. Baseline commit `09b24a0c269dbc07942e87741b112e848115165e`; working universe name VelocityVerse, first season **The Openline**. Everything described as proposed is design work, not delivered functionality, a uniqueness claim or a promise of commercial results.

## What differentiated product should connect racing, engineering, characters and belonging?

### Takeaway

Proposed player promise: **“Your best lap matters to someone.”** Build a small motorsport story in which a run produces evidence, evidence changes a crew decision, and the decision leaves a visible memory in the place players call home. A large feature count is less defensible than a convincing emotional and mechanical connection between four people, one car and one circuit.

### Inferences

#### Proposed core experience

The player joins the Openline Garage in Lumen Harbor, where public engineering knowledge is becoming restricted by Crownline's proprietary championship rules. No one saves a universe with magic speed. A crew must prove that an old community workshop can run a safe, credible public driving school. The player learns that a fast result can be incomplete evidence and that helping a rival can be a competitive strength.

The first 10–15 minutes establish four people through actions: **Neri Vale**, impulsive test driver, needs a patient teammate; **Tavi Pell**, tactile model-builder, needs someone willing to test a hypothesis; **Maela Rook**, retired champion and garage steward, must acknowledge an old compromised test; **Sola Kest**, funded Crownline champion, chooses between protecting an advantage and acknowledging good evidence. The player is an original recruit, separate from the cast, and can choose a guide rather than a superior-stat hero. Character expertise changes explanations and mission perspective, never paid vehicle performance.

Repeatable session: arrive at garage and see one consequence of last visit → choose a 2–5 minute crew commission → drive a controlled trial or short race → inspect one understandable comparison → make one authored crew decision → receive a visual memory, mastery milestone or story page. A 15–25 minute chapter can contain several sessions and save at each phase. Racing remains enjoyable without reading; story pages remain available without replaying a grind.

The distinctive **hypothesis**, to validate rather than advertise as unique, is that a model-generated trace becomes both a learning object and a relationship object. Tavi remembers that you admitted uncertainty; Sola recognizes that your published test included conditions; the garage displays the beacon the crew restored. The objective is recognition and competence, not a map full of lock icons.

#### Modules A–G as one ecosystem

| Module | First playable scope | Connected value | Expansion gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Racing | One Harbor Loop, one shared-spec VX-1; existing practice/TT/AI races plus a short authored homologation run | Produce valid evidence and mastery moments; reset story challenges freely | Weather requires actual grip, visibility and AI work; endurance needs wear/energy/pits; online needs authority and fairness |
| B Engineering | Existing isolated grip/aero comparisons, plus one controlled setup choice for the story trial | Predict, test and explain a tradeoff; never label every part an upgrade | Chassis/engine/electric/suspension building only after reliable parameter validation and comprehensible feedback |
| C Team management | Four authored NPC crew members, garage identity and one finite project allocation | Choose whose idea to test and what the community exhibits | Hiring/resources/sponsors require decisions with distinct outcomes, not idle timers; no real employment simulation claim |
| D Character progression | One trust flag per relationship, authored acknowledgement, mission journal | Remember an action and invite a different viewpoint next time | More branches require a bounded content graph, localization budget and consistency tests |
| E Social gaming | NPC crew and optional locally exported achievement card, explicitly offline | A team experience can be demonstrated without pretending other humans are present | Real friend crews/invitations, garage visits/spectating/events require accounts, permissions, moderation and support |
| F Creator ecosystem | Personal paint already exists; first creator experiment is a local challenge assembled from approved objectives | Express identity without needing to build a game engine | Curated publishing of challenge codes before routes; arbitrary scripting, public vehicle imports and marketplace come much later |
| G Education | Prediction → controlled measurement → explanation → fresh transfer challenge | Learn a principle because a character's project needs it | School edition follows educator validation; certificates never imply accredited engineering competence |

Different vehicle categories should express different decisions rather than increasing a linear power score: compact electric sprint (energy recovery), open-wheel club car (aero), closed-wheel endurance car (consistency), lightweight utility trial vehicle (load/material choices). First playable delivers only the existing open-wheel category. All racing takes place on closed, sanctioned circuits; youth-facing hidden challenges involve observation, clean lines and engineering, not evading police or danger on public streets.

#### Science mission coverage

Each topic has a future mission that can be checked with an independent transfer question. Aerodynamics: trade braking stability against neutral coasting. Newton's laws: distinguish force, acceleration and mass using controlled starts. Friction: compare braking on measured surface parameters. Energy: budget an electric stint and recover energy under braking. Materials: choose an explicitly simplified mass/stiffness/cost combination. Mathematics: predict a speed-time area and compare distance. Probability: choose a strategy from a displayed weather distribution, then review expected versus realized outcome. Electronics: connect a bounded sensor circuit and diagnose a missing signal. Sustainability: compare scoped in-game material and energy budgets, disclosing assumptions rather than presenting a carbon footprint as a real lifecycle assessment. Only friction/aero/braking have substantial prototype foundations today.

#### Thirty mechanic proposals

Labels: **E established mechanic**, **C meaningful combination**, **H novel hypothesis**. H means the specific proposed interaction needs research and tests; it does not mean nobody has tried it. Effort estimates concern a believable prototype with a small team, excluding full art, localization and operations: S≈1–2 engineering weeks, M≈4–8 multidisciplinary weeks, L≈2–4 months, XL≥6 months/service obligations. These are planning assumptions, not commitments. P1=first 90-day slice; P2=months 4–6 after gates; P3=months 7–12 conditional; P4=beyond year one. A competitor analogue documents a component; it does not prove equivalent quality or exact feature parity. “No verified exact analogue” means no claim of absence across the market.

**01 Evidence lap — C; P1; M.** What/how: one run creates a versioned speed/brake trace and a single actionable comparison chosen by the player. Difference: completing the crew's question matters, even when the lap is slower. Analogue: GT7 sector practice/ghosts are established [GT7 Circuit Experience](https://www.gran-turismo.com/us/gt7/manual/worldcircuit/04); the trace-to-character consequence is a proposed combination. Technology: simulation events, fixed-condition replay, small trace store. Return: test a new explanation. Revenue: optional authored chapter packs; core feedback remains free. Test: at least 8/12 observed beginners can explain why the second run changed; reject a pure stat screen.

**02 Crew promise — H; P1; S.** What/how: before a run promise Neri “clean return” or Tavi “repeat the test”; actual events determine the acknowledgement. Difference: a promise concerns behavior, not winning or dialog flattery. Analogue: F1's authored character story is established [Braking Point](https://www.ea.com/en/games/f1/f1-25/news/f1-25-braking-point); exact evidence-bound promise unknown. Technology: authored predicate and durable one-time event. Return: experience another crew perspective without trust decay. Revenue: story expansions, never paid reconciliation. Test: players correctly predict which driving action fulfills the promise; remove if it feels like a hidden morality meter.

**03 Mentor fade — C; P1; M.** What/how: Maela offers one braking cue; after demonstrated mastery, the player can remove that cue and test transfer. Difference: removing help is optional, and assists stay labeled in records. Analogue: GT7 assists and sector practice [GT7 race menu](https://www.gran-turismo.com/us/gt7/manual/race/01). Technology: mastery observations, cue visibility, explanation templates. Return: a satisfying self-chosen challenge. Revenue: free core learning; paid cosmetic mentor workshop themes. Test: compare cue-free success after training with an equal-time unguided practice group; do not infer learning from completion alone.

**04 Openline recipe — C; P2; M.** What/how: attach setup, conditions, model version and a trace to a shareable engineering recipe. Difference: a dramatic claim must include repeatable conditions. Analogue: Forza shares event/prefab content [EventLab](https://forza.net/news/forza-horizon-5-horizon-creatives); exact evidence contract not verified. Technology: signed manifest, compatibility checks, bounded parameter schema, moderation. Return: reproduce a friend's finding. Revenue: creator cosmetic packaging later; recipes free. Test: 95% of compatible valid recipes reproduce within their published tolerance and invalid imports never modify race configuration.

**05 Garage remembers — C; P1; M.** What/how: a restored beacon, repaired workbench or handwritten test card visibly persists after a chapter. Difference: the display records an attributable choice rather than purchased decoration alone. Analogue: Fortnite persistence [UEFN](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/fortnite/persistence-devices-in-unreal-editor-for-fortnite), Forza's custom garages [FH6 FAQ](https://support.forza.net/hc/en-us/articles/48409181275539-Forza-Horizon-6-FAQ). Technology: small scene-state bitset, save migrations. Return: revisit a place with personal history. Revenue: additional decoration styles without changing earned memory. Test: most returning testers notice and recall the specific cause unaided; do not build whole districts before this works.

**06 Rival reads one habit — H; P2; L.** What/how: Sola's authored race AI selects among three fair responses to a player's prior braking or passing tendency. Difference: a bounded, legible adaptation with a post-race explanation; no secret power boost. Analogue: no verified exact analogue in sources reviewed. Technology: replay features, constrained tactical state machine, same physics, difficulty caps. Return: adjust strategy against a recognizable rival. Revenue: character chapters, no sale of AI weakness. Test: blinded drivers distinguish adaptation from randomness above chance without reporting cheating; fail if tactics cannot be audited.

**07 Clean-air pact — H; P2; M.** What/how: a solo story trial asks the player to leave a safe gap for Neri's measurement, then trade roles with an NPC. Difference: cooperation is measurable distance and consistency, not a “teamwork” button. Analogue: iRacing has shared team roles, a broader precedent [Team Racing](https://support.iracing.com/support/solutions/articles/31000173707-team-racing); exact pact unverified. Technology: track projection, distance windows, contacts and NPC logic. Return: improve a joint clean trace. Revenue: cosmetics only. Test: players understand a valid gap and failures never incentivize dangerous blocking.

**08 Five-minute relay — C; P3; L.** What/how: friends contribute separate short standardized stints to one asynchronous team entry. Difference: no one must remain online for a long endurance event; contribution is capped and late absence carries no social debt. Analogue: multi-driver iRacing team races are established [Team Racing](https://support.iracing.com/support/solutions/articles/31000173707-team-racing). Technology: validated replays, team identity, server scoring, equal-condition manifests. Return: see complementary contributions. Revenue: cosmetic crew packs, no paid extra scoring stints. Test: mixed-schedule crews complete an entry without pressure; audit equal scoring across assist classes.

**09 One-variable workshop — E; P1; S extension.** What/how: hold conditions fixed and change exactly one permitted parameter. Difference: honest tradeoff and provenance rather than an upgrade arrow. Analogue: experimental science lessons already exist [Minecraft Science](https://education.minecraft.net/en-us/resources/science). Technology: current isolated Vehicle lab plus authored prediction/feedback. Return: resolve curiosity. Revenue: educator tools later, no sale of correct answers. Test: transfer a principle to a previously unseen setup rather than repeat the displayed result.

**10 Finite bench budget — C; P2; M.** What/how: allocate a fixed non-purchasable project budget among mass, grip and cooling, with common championship rules. Difference: resources reset per challenge, so spending cannot dominate. Analogue: no exact current analogue verified here; vehicle development/management is an established design family. Technology: validated setup solver, bounded configuration UI, scoring classes. Return: discover distinct viable solutions. Revenue: chassis skins; no budget top-ups. Test: at least three competitive configurations survive automated parameter sweeps and players explain their chosen compromise.

**11 Energy delivery cup — C; P3; L.** What/how: run a closed-circuit electric challenge delivering a fictional energy reserve while meeting consistency constraints. Difference: shortest time and greatest reserve form a visible tradeoff. Analogue: sustainability/engineering worlds exist [Minecraft Science](https://education.minecraft.net/en-us/resources/science); exact motorsport format unverified. Technology: actual electric powertrain, energy accounting, calibrated simplifications. Return: improve a Pareto frontier. Revenue: story/cosmetic content. Test: energy integrates consistently from power, and players can beat a fresh challenge using the learned relationship.

**12 Materials passport — C; P3; L.** What/how: record a fictional part's mass, repairability, stiffness and reuse history; compare scoped costs. Difference: ownership is a transparent story, not a scarcity token. Analogue: engineering education precedent [Minecraft Science](https://education.minecraft.net/en-us/resources/science); exact passport unverified. Technology: authored part catalog, simplified structural models, provenance tags. Return: try a repair-first solution. Revenue: physical maker kits after safety review; no blockchain needed. Test: players distinguish simulated tradeoffs from real materials certification and real-world climate claims.

**13 Storm rehearsal — E; P3; L.** What/how: test two forecast scenarios before a wet race, then choose tires or conservative driving. Difference: disclose uncertainty and preserve a dry accessibility route. Analogue: F1 25 features racing conditions [EA features](https://www.ea.com/games/f1/f1-25/features); current repository wet appearance is visual only. Technology: surface water/grip, weather, AI recalibration, forecast model. Return: adapt an understandable plan. Revenue: included gameplay; weather-themed cosmetics. Test: wet and dry braking differ coherently; forecasts are calibrated across repeated seeded cases.

**14 Archive counterfactual — C; P3; L.** What/how: replay an authored historic race with an alternative legal engineering constraint and label the result a simulation. Difference: distinguish canon fact, reconstruction and player “what if.” Analogue: no verified exact analogue in this research. Technology: versioned snapshots, compatible legacy simulation, story labeling. Return: explore Maela's disputed choice. Revenue: archive chapter packs. Test: players remember which outcome is canonical and understand why the simulated change mattered.

**15 Comparable challenge passport — C; P2; M.** What/how: attach assist/control/model/track/condition metadata to every trial, preventing misleading comparisons. Difference: celebrate personal improvement without claiming a keyboard, tilt and auto-brake time are identical competition. Analogue: GT7 exposes assist/ghost settings [GT7 race menu](https://www.gran-turismo.com/us/gt7/manual/race/01), not proof of identical grouping policy. Technology: immutable run manifest, class scoring, UI disclosure. Return: fair attainable goals. Revenue: none directly; trust supports the product. **16 Crew roles with equal dignity — C; P2; M.** What/how: driver, tester and observer each contribute a bounded project task; no role grants faster vehicles. Difference: players with different confidence can matter without specialist equipment. Analogue: customizable garages/team identity are established [FH6 FAQ](https://support.forza.net/hc/en-us/articles/48409181275539-Forza-Horizon-6-FAQ); exact living crest unverified. Difference: expectations are explicit before joining, not inferred from rank. **19 Shared hypothesis board — C; P3; L.** What/how: friends choose from approved engineering hypotheses and contribute compatible test traces asynchronously. Analogue: FH6 collaborative creation [FH6 FAQ](https://support.forza.net/hc/en-us/articles/48409181275539-Forza-Horizon-6-FAQ); exact hypothesis workflow unverified. ### Gaps

No human evidence currently establishes character attachment, return behavior, teaching effectiveness, fun relative to competitors or the value of trace-linked consequences. No exact analogue search can establish worldwide novelty. The current source and inherited reports do not justify production-readiness or a sustained 60 FPS claim; older documentation contains no-device statements superseded by subsequent phone installation in the conversation. Multiplayer, payment, creator services, cloud saves and story characters remain absent in the inspected commit.

## How should the Android experience look, move and navigate?

### Takeaway

Proposed visual identity: **a luminous, tactile public workshop overlooking a living harbor**, with a race trace as its recurring graphic motif. Use cinematic scene composition, character performance and material contrast for spectacle; keep phone navigation stable and immediate. A page of metric cards is not the universe.

### Cited findings

- Android recommends at least 48×48dp touch targets and suitable spacing. [Android accessibility guidance](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/views/apps-views), [Google target-size guidance](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/7101858?hl=en).
- Microsoft's game accessibility guidance covers disabling or pausing nonessential motion and providing camera/motion options. [Xbox accessibility guideline117](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/117).
- Android documents thermal feedback/workload adaptation and frame pacing, and recommends preserving UI clarity when reducing scene resolution. These are implementation options, not measured performance of this prototype. [Android game optimization](https://developer.android.com/games/optimize), [Android power efficiency](https://developer.android.com/games/optimize/power).
- The current host already packages local content, supports Android Back, handles application pause, offers native haptics/exports and can keep the screen awake. This is a usable prototype base, not proof that all interactions are tested on every phone. [MainActivity](../../android/app/src/main/java/com/velocityverse/game/MainActivity.java).

### Inferences

#### Design system proposal

**Composition:** the inhabited garage is home. A character is welding a safe closed test fixture, a model is on Tavi's bench, the beacon is visible outside. Each is a destination with a clear label and a static list alternative. The camera is a scene transition, never a locomotion obligation. Landscape home has a strong car/crew focal point, with thumb-accessible play dock. Portrait home preserves that focal point with one “Continue crew mission” action; it does not shrink six tiny landscape cards into illegibility.

**Palette:** deep ink `#080D1B`, ceramic `#E7ECE7`, signal cyan `#48DDE1`, warm coral `#FF786B`, task amber `#FFD66A`, reserved character violet `#A699E8`. Neon is emissive edge/detail light, not the fill of every text surface. Environment saturation rises near meaningful actions. Race hazards use a stable danger language; don't reuse danger red for rewards. State always has icon/text/pattern as well as color. Proposed text contrast targets4.5:1 normal/3:1 large, to be measured against actual composites.

**Typography:** one expressive original/licensed display face for 2–5-word labels, one highly readable UI family for body text and numerals. Existing Barlow Condensed/Inter provide a low-cost licensed prototype pairing; brand typography requires readability trials rather than an expensive font promise. Proposed base body16sp and labels14sp; no uppercase paragraphs. Keep driver/license data tabular. Support 130% and200% text without truncating actions; use reflow/scrolling detail sheets at enlarged text.

**Material:** ceramic garage tags, brushed aluminum, translucent test overlays and weathered painted surfaces. Rounded functional touch controls inside sharply cut environmental frames. A frame can be angular without turning every button into a difficult polygon. High bloom and fake floating currency rows are not measures of premium quality.

**Navigation:** four stable roots—Home, Crew, Garage, World. Race/Continue is a prominent contextual action, not a fifth always-selected root. Profile/settings in the same top position; Academy is inside Garage, Engineering on Tavi's bench with a direct shortcut, stories in Crew, competition in World. Android Back dismisses keyboard, then sheet/dialog, then returns along the actual stack; in play it pauses. Root exit remains native. Hide social/creator/shop entry points until a real usable feature is available; a design bible can specify future screens without putting dead buttons into the first app.

**Orientation:** portrait for home, character/story/profile and casual garage browsing; landscape for driving, test traces, detailed circuit editing and optional showroom. Complete a deliberate transition before starting a clock. Save current state before rotation; pause on interruption. Never animate orientation while a vehicle is moving. Offer landscape shell to players who cannot rotate comfortably, with layout parity. Handle cutouts, gesture insets, display-size changes and foldables without covering controls.

**Motion:** touch feedback80–120ms; ordinary sheet180–240ms; deliberate scene cut300–450ms; one focal event on chapter completion. No screen shake while reading, auto-scrolling dialog, pulsing expired offers or simultaneous moving backgrounds under dense text. “Reduced motion” freezes scenic motion, replaces camera flights with a crossfade and preserves immediate status feedback. All dialogue advances manually; subtitles and a complete transcript exist. Haptics are short and respect system controls.

**Proposed budgets to validate:** root input acknowledgement≤100ms p95, warm destination transition≤500ms p95, cold playable content≤10s on named baseline, cached chapter resume≤3s; menu scene cap30fps or static battery mode, racing aim60fps with honest30fps supported fallback. Keep UI at display resolution while lowering scene resolution. Proposed scene-only memory≤350MB on midrange6GB test phone; whole-process PSS≤650MB, to revise from profiling rather than assume. Pause all menu animation when obscured/backgrounded. Report p50/p95/p99 frame times, stall counts, thermal headroom, memory and battery over20-minute sessions; a screenshot proves none of these.

#### UX acceptance review

Use actual phone hands and actual text sizes, not only desktop screenshots. The review set must include a360dp-wide portrait device, short landscape device, notch/gesture device,200%text, color-vision simulation, TalkBack on every menu, reduced motion, silent audio and one-handed interaction. Track accidental taps, path completion time and comprehension. Ask “show me where to continue Neri's project,” not “does this look premium?” Test an interruption mid-story and mid-race, storage failure, offline state, malformed export/import and long translated labels. Existing automated bounds/screenshots are useful regression evidence; they cannot establish tactile comfort, character appeal or visual superiority.

No production art, character performance, animation rig, touch usability study, accessibility audit or performance capture establishes these specifications. ## What first playable and roadmap can a small team prove?

### Takeaway

Ship a complete **“Light the Beacon”** vertical slice before building a live universe: four characters, one garage, Harbor Loop, one car, a measured experiment, a consequential crew choice and a durable visible result. A believable offline crew is a valid bounded team experience; it does not satisfy the later promise of playing with real friends.

### Cited findings

- The current source already provides the fixed-step Vehicle model, timing/rules, local saves, academy checks, a lab and native package hooks that the slice could reuse. No new code was implemented as part of these design notes. ### Inferences

#### Mission: Light the Beacon

**Premise:** the retired harbor timing beacon can reopen Openline Garage's public driving school if the crew submits a reproducible safe test and completes a sanctioned homologation run. The authority isn't asking children to repair public infrastructure or drive on streets; all physical work is represented by qualified fictional staff, and the player tests a car on a closed circuit.

**Beat1, belonging (45–90s):** Maela gives the player a chipped ceramic test tag. Neri wants to run immediately; Tavi asks for a measured braking baseline. Sola arrives to witness the homologation. Player picks their guide and cosmetic identity; none changes physics. Two optional sentences establish Crownline's proposed enclosure without a lore wall.

**Beat2, prediction (30–60s):** Tavi offers baseline and one fixed experimental setup in an isolated trial. Player predicts “shorter stopping distance,” “longer,” or “unsure”; a wrong prediction does not lose points or trust. The lab uses actual identical entry speed and conditions, no hard-coded triumphant result.

**Beat3, measurement (2–3min):** accelerate into a bounded target window, brake inside a marked test area, come to a stable stop. Measure distance from the first qualified entry-speed event to sustained stop. Entry outside the window requests a retry, clearly explaining comparability. Teleports/resets, wrong direction, nonfinite state and barrier collisions invalidate this evidence, with immediate feedback and no punishment. The manual mastery variant requires a valid driver brake input. An assisted story variant offers a clearly labeled supported measurement and an accessible prediction/observation task; it must not block the chapter because a player cannot make a manual input. Rookie help remains available and is labeled.

**Beat4, honest crew choice (1–2min):** Tavi's setup improves one measure but is not universally better. Choose to publish the comparison with its limitations, or run a repeat before submitting. Both are defensible; repeated testing can reveal reproducibility, not farm trust. Neri initially resists the delay but later repeats a cue the player practiced. If the player guesses a claim unsupported by the trace, Maela explains uncertainty and permits correction; no irreversible shame branch.

**Beat5, corner and homologation (3–5min):** first a short clean-corner task checks ordered progress, actual steering, bounded slip and no contacts; then a two-lap fixed-spec exhibition against Sola and other NPCs on Harbor Loop. Homologation requires completion and a clean qualified segment, not first place. Sola gives racing room because they are a credible rival, not a rubber-band villain. Optional mastery goal: beat one's own comparable clean segment. No purchased setup or XP modifies performance. Auto-throttle/auto-brake outcomes belong to the assisted class; story continuation remains accessible.

**Beat6, memory (1–2min):** a known mission state lights the beacon and places the signed test card on the garage wall. Sola acknowledges evidence; Maela reveals she once omitted a test condition, opening the next chapter. Team reputation receives one authored public-school permit, not an invented global rank. Reward: a permanent cosmetic beacon stripe, a journal page and one first-completion mastery marker. The next voluntary invitation asks the player to help Neri explain the test to a new recruit, establishing return value rather than an expiring daily reward.

**Failure/retry:** keep story choices, restart only the failed driving stage. Never delete relationships, charge fuel, wait for energy or show a paid skip. Free practice always available. Exact driving thresholds must be calibrated through beginner sessions; the above duration windows are design assumptions.

**Minimum state/data contract:** `missionId`, `missionVersion`, `phase`, `attemptId`, vehicle/track/model/assist manifest, validated objective event IDs, one-time reward ledger, four bounded authored relationship flags, chosen evidence/claim, garage memory IDs, last safe checkpoint and settings. Use stable IDs with migrations and atomic save boundaries. Retrying/reopening cannot duplicate rewards; updates cannot silently turn completed missions incomplete. Local data is tamperable; server validation is a separate later task. A first-time storage failure tells the player progress is session-only and provides a supported recovery path; do not promise cloud backup.

**Audience and research gate:** 13+ is a proposed creative audience, not a legal classification or exemption. Start any account-based India research pilot with consenting adults18+; include teens only after guardian, jurisdictional legal and safety gates are implemented. Offline usability research also needs an appropriate research/guardian consent process. Do not infer eligibility from a store rating.

**Four-character delivery floor:** each appears through one distinctive action, one conflict and one remembered callback, with silhouette-readable portrait, short performance or illustrated animation and subtitles. Four selectable names with identical avatar bodies would not satisfy the brief. Full animation/cinematic production can be staged: high-quality illustrated performances first, consistent modeled assets when pipeline proven.

#### Founder objections and decisions

Reject for first release:10 modeled cities,18 playable character campaigns, arbitrary AI stories, open voice/text chat, tradable economy, real-money creator payouts, persistent shared open world, real-time endurance, licensed F1 roster, procedural infinite missions, school procurement and merchandise inventory. They require different teams and multiply quality/safety/content risk before demand exists.

The hardest product problem is making the crew consequence intrinsically rewarding after the novelty of a polished screen fades. The hardest first technical problem is sustained controllable driving and reliable saves on actual affordable Android hardware; later the difficult platform problem is authoritative multiplayer plus trusted, safe creator content, not adding a chat model. Biggest commercial risk: a middling racer carrying expensive narrative overhead; teens may prefer their existing social games and racers may not want a tutorial. Test those segments separately rather than average their conflicting needs.

Two disciplined options after alpha: if driving is loved but characters ignored, sell a focused original racer and pause franchise expansion; if characters are loved but driving abandoned, simplify driving and test a crew/engineering adventure. If neither earns voluntary return, reject the premise rather than build a second world. This is a potentially valuable original product thesis; current evidence does not establish a billion-dollar category or technical/visual leadership.

### Gaps

The roadmap has no committed staffing, budget, art throughput, validated demand, statistically powered cohort or engine decision. No first playable story is implemented by this research task. A parent/family governance model, country-specific compliance and production server/security work require the adjacent strategy research. The first playable's offline NPC crew does not prove human community retention, safe invitations or multiplayer quality. These must remain named gates in any public pitch.
